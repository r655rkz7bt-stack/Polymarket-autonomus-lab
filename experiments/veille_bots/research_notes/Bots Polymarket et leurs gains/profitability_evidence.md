# Who makes money on Polymarket, and how: academic and on-chain evidence (2024-2026)

Research date: 2026-10-16. All web content treated as data. "Verified" below means I read the primary document (paper PDF/abstract or the original analysis page) myself; "secondary" means I only saw it via a press summary or search snippet. Several primary pages (Dune blog, The Defiant, Bloomberg, Galaxy/bonus.com) were blocked by Cloudflare or paywalls, so their numbers come from secondary coverage and are flagged as such.

## Q1. Academic papers: exact findings and numbers

### Takeaway
Four academic studies converge: (a) Polymarket prices are broadly accurate but show a favourite-longshot bias (longshots overpriced, favourites underpriced); (b) gains are extremely concentrated, and the winners are mostly **liquidity providers using limit orders**, while losers are **takers using market orders** and longshot buyers; (c) pure arbitrage (YES+NO < $1, cross-market inconsistencies) extracted ~$40M in one year (Apr 2024-Apr 2025), dominated by bot-like accounts; (d) ~25% of historical volume looks like wash trading (airdrop farming), which inflates volume and the count of "losing" wallets.

### Cited Findings

**Saguillo, Ghafouri, Kiffer, Suarez-Tangil, "Unravelling the Probabilistic Forest: Arbitrage in Prediction Markets" (IMDEA Networks / Oxford Internet Institute; arXiv 2508.03474, 5 Aug 2025; AFT 2025). Verified from the PDF.**
- Sample: markets resolved 1 Apr 2024 - 1 Apr 2025; 8,659 single-condition markets + 1,578 multi-condition (NegRisk) markets containing 8,559 conditions = 17,218 conditions; on-chain data from 1 Jan 2024 to 1 Apr 2025; 86 million bids (bids < $2 filtered out). — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.03474)
- 7,051 of 17.2K conditions (2,628 NegRisk, 4,423 single) had at least one arbitrage opportunity under their parameters (opportunities only counted if ≥ $0.05 profit per dollar and no token > $0.95). Median sum of YES+NO prices in those opportunities ≈ $0.60 ("remarkable market inefficiency"). — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.03474)
- Realised arbitrage, single-condition (buy YES+NO below $1 / sell above $1): $5,899,287 from buying below $1, $4,682,075 from selling above $1. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.03474)
- Realised arbitrage within/between markets (NegRisk rebalancing): buying YES $11,092,286; selling YES $612,189; selling NO $4,264; **buying NO $17,307,114** (the largest bucket; the authors note Polymarket itself tweeted that some accounts profit "just by buying NO"). Politics dominated (US election, Aug 2024 Democratic nominee/VP markets); Sports largely absent. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.03474)
- Combinatorial (cross-market) arbitrage was tiny: 13 dependent US-election market pairs identified with LLM help; executed arbitrage in 5 of 11 eligible pairs, totals $60,237 / $18,472 / $15,819 / $629. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.03474)
- Total across strategies: **$39,587,585** (assuming ε = $1 profit per trade). Top account earned **$2,009,632 over 4,049 transactions** (≈ $496/trade); top-10 accounts earned $2.01M, $1.27M, $1.09M, $769K, $750K, $538K, $477K, $468K, $425K, $384K with 200-4,533 transactions each. Authors describe "very big players with bot-like behaviour in the number of bids". — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.03474)
- Outlier: user @Tutaaa91 bought both YES and NO for < $0.02 each, single-trade profit $58,983. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.03474)
- Caveat: secondary coverage (TechFlow) says IMDEA "shows the largest profits accrue to... arbitrage bots, market-making algorithms and HFT"; the paper itself only says "bot-like behaviour" and leaves strategy study to future work. — [TechFlow](https://www.techflowpost.com/en-US/article/31018)

**Akey (ESSEC), Grégoire (HEC Montréal), Harvie & Martineau (U. Toronto), "Who Wins and Who Loses in Prediction Markets? Evidence from Polymarket", CEPR Discussion Paper 21615 (2026). Abstract verified; detailed numbers secondary.**
- Abstract (current version): 588 million trades, $67 billion volume; top 1% of users with positive PnL capture **76.5% of profits**; "successful traders provide liquidity using limit orders that resolve favorably relative to realized outcomes while unsuccessful traders take liquidity using market orders"; monthly performance "modestly persistent, however this may represent sample selection rather than skill"; insider trading "unlikely to explain the performance of the largest winners". — [EconPapers abstract](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:cpr:ceprdp:21615)
- Earlier version (Nov 2022 - Oct 2025; 1.4M users, 70M trades, > $20B volume) as summarised by InGame: **70.8% of users in the red**; top 0.1% of profitable users take 58.5% of gains, top 1% 84.1%, top 5% 95.8%, top 25% 99.6%. Prices are well calibrated (30¢ contracts happen ~30% of the time). Top 2% of traders' contracts resolve 8-15¢ above purchase price ("excess hit rate"); worst ~15¢ below. Probit: moving from pure taker to pure maker **reduces probability of losing by ~36 percentage points**. Users place **63% of trades at extreme prices (<10¢ or >90¢)** and longshot buying is a losing profile. Authors: "Polymarket investors are, on average, trading against efficient prices they cannot profit from." Multi-wallet use means real concentration is likely higher. — [InGame summary](https://www.ingame.com/?p=48488)
- Note the numbers differ between versions (76.5% vs 84.1% for top 1%; different samples). Press (Bloomberg-derived) quotes "around 69% lost money since 2022, top 1% captured three-quarters of profits". — [Search summary of Bloomberg coverage via The Edge](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/801797) (page returned 404 when fetched; secondary)

**Columbia Business School wash-trading study (Allen Sirolly et al.; SSRN, 6-7 Nov 2025; not peer reviewed). Secondary (Decrypt, Fortune).**
- ~**25% of Polymarket's historical volume** consistent with wash trading; suspicious patterns in **14% of 1.26 million wallets**; ~$4.5 billion of trades likely wash. — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/347842/columbia-study-25-polymarket-volume-wash-trading)
- Time profile: peaked at nearly **60% of weekly volume in Dec 2024**, under 5% by May 2025, ~20% by Oct 2025. By category: Sports 45% of all-time volume, Election 17%, Politics 12%, **Crypto 3%**; peaks of 95% (Election, week of 24 Mar 2025) and 90% (Sports, week of 21 Oct 2024). — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/347842/columbia-study-25-polymarket-volume-wash-trading); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/11/07/polymarket-wash-trading-inflated-prediction-markets-columbia-research)
- Drivers cited by the authors: no KYC, no transaction fees (at the time), anticipation of a token airdrop. Sirolly: "Wash trading does not require large amounts of capital"; "no evidence that the exchange is involved". — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/347842/columbia-study-25-polymarket-volume-wash-trading)

**Calibration / favourite-longshot bias**
- Walker (Princeton ORFE senior thesis, 9 Apr 2026): 188,509 resolved binary markets, 1,875,570 daily price observations, Nov 2022 - Dec 2025. Brier Skill Score 0.398 overall (Sports 0.264 worst, Politics 0.565 best). Logistic recalibration slope b = 1.112 → underconfidence consistent with favourite-longshot bias: "prices systematically compressed toward 0.50, with longshots overpriced and favorites underpriced"; present in all domains and worse at longer horizons. Undergraduate thesis (not peer reviewed). — [Princeton Thesis Central](https://theses-dissertations.princeton.edu/entities/publication/d5708372-5ea3-4252-b669-7f9cee07387c)
- Le (2026), arXiv 2602.19520: 292 million trades across 327,000 binary contracts on Kalshi and Polymarket; persistent underconfidence in political markets (prices compressed toward 50%) generalises across both exchanges; Kalshi's trade-size effect (Δ = 0.53) does not replicate on Polymarket (Δ = 0.11, CI [-0.15, 0.39]). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.19520v1)
- Structural "No" bias: Polymarket publishes that 73.3% of resolved markets end "No"; Decrypt's own sample of 2,300+ closed positions: 90-180-day markets resolve No 73.5%, < 1-week markets ~52%. — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/364381/polymarket-bot-bets-no-has-a-point)

### Inferences
- The academic picture is "accurate prices + small systematic biases": the edge available to a generic predictor is small; the robust edges are (1) being the maker rather than the taker, (2) selling longshots / buying favourites (FLB), (3) mechanical arbitrage of YES+NO and NegRisk sums, and (4) buying "No" on long-dated "will X happen by date" markets.
- The arbitrage paper's $40M is over a period including the 2024 US election; the median-$0.60 opportunities were mostly in thin, illiquid conditions, so capacity per opportunity is small (authors bound profit with $100 liquidity scenarios).
- Wash trading (25% of volume, 14% of wallets) mechanically inflates the count of small losing wallets, so "% of wallets that lose" overstates the share of genuine traders that lose — but it does not affect the profit-concentration conclusion.

### Gaps
- Could not read the full Martineau et al. paper PDF or Columbia SSRN PDF (only abstract/summaries); exact author list of the Columbia paper beyond Sirolly not confirmed.
- Reichenbach & Walther (2025, TU Berlin / German International University) ~70% losers of ~2M users: only seen in secondary coverage ([Digital Today](https://www.digitaltoday.co.kr/en/view/49006/polymarket-traders-84-percent-lose-money-in-prediction-market)); not verified.
- Bürgi et al. (2025) favourite-longshot study is on Kalshi, not Polymarket (CEPR/VoxEU page blocked).
- No academic paper found that specifically measures market-maker inventory/spread behaviour on Polymarket.

## Q2. On-chain analyses of top wallets and bots: strategies, profits, trade counts, holding times

### Takeaway
The best-documented bot battleground is the 5-/15-minute crypto Up/Down markets. Verified on-chain data (Telonex, 1 week Feb 2026) shows makers net positive while takers lose despite being "right" more often; profitable archetypes are (a) 100%-maker quoting bots across thousands of markets, (b) "snipers" making a few large, late, well-timed bets (likely using Binance/Coinbase spot feeds), and (c) hybrids. Named-wallet profit claims in the press ($200K/month etc.) are mostly unverified.

### Cited Findings

- Data: every resolved 15-minute crypto market 2-8 Feb 2026 — 2,688 markets (672 per coin: BTC, ETH, SOL, XRP), 15.3M on-chain fills, $153M volume (single-counted), 46,945 wallets, avg 5,711 fills and $56,763 volume per market. PnL is **pre-fee** (taker fees and maker rebates not included). Reproducible notebook on GitHub. — [Telonex](https://telonex.io/research/top-crypto-traders-polymarket-15m)
- **Only 36.8% of wallets profitable**; median wallet PnL ≈ -$3. Top wallet +$270,569 in the week; top 5 combined $811,403; biggest loser -$139,971 (a majority-maker wallet across 142 markets, "likely suffered from adverse selection"). 19 of top 20 wallets primarily trade BTC. — [Telonex](https://telonex.io/research/top-crypto-traders-polymarket-15m)
- **Maker-taker paradox**: takers win 53% of individual fills but lose overall; makers win only 47% of fills but net **+$728,501** for the week (pre-fee; after fees/rebates the gap widens). — [Telonex](https://telonex.io/research/top-crypto-traders-polymarket-15m)
- Intra-market timing: fill count highest at minute 0, but **volume spikes in the final minutes and concentrates at extreme prices near $0 and $1**. — [Telonex](https://telonex.io/research/top-crypto-traders-polymarket-15m)

### Inferences
- Holding times: in fast markets, positions are held minutes (≤ 15 min) and typically to resolution (gabigol: hold-to-redeem; Telonex: volume concentrated in final minutes). Arbitrage positions in the IMDEA study are held to resolution (mint/merge or redeem). Market makers hold inventory within a single 15-min window.
- The documented profitable automated patterns are: (1) passive two-sided quoting with maker rebates (verified by Telonex + Martineau); (2) late-window "convergence" buying of 94-99¢ favourites using external spot feeds (plausible, partly evidenced by Telonex's volume-at-extremes and sniper wallets); (3) YES+NO / NegRisk-sum arbitrage (verified historically by IMDEA, but fee introduction in 2026 shrinks it in fee-bearing markets).
- Since Jan 2026, taker fees (highest near 50¢) are charged in fast crypto markets, so the pre-fee Telonex taker results are an upper bound for taker strategies.

### Gaps
- No audited, fee-adjusted P&L for any named bot (distinct-baguette, sharky6999, ilovecircle, gabagool). The $242K for distinct-baguette appears only in one Yellow.com article without a wallet address.
- The Bloomberg article (28 Apr 2026) with named bot examples was not readable (paywall); only Newser/FinanceMagnates summaries.
- Exact current fee curve (claimed max 1.56% effective) seen only in an unverified search snippet; check Polymarket docs.
- WSJ reporting on Théo's private "neighbour effect" polls not re-verified in this session.

## Q3. Share of traders who lose, share of volume that is bots, profit concentration

### Takeaway
Every study finds a majority of wallets lose: ~63% (one week of 15m crypto) to ~70% (academic, Dec-2025 on-chain) to 84% (Apr 2026, all wallets incl. splits/merges). Profits are hyper-concentrated: top 1% of winners take 76-84% of profits; ~0.03-0.04% of addresses take most realised profit. Bots are ~5% of wallets but ~75% of volume (Bloomberg) and 55-62% of volume in 5/15-min crypto markets (Dune).

### Cited Findings
- Bloomberg (Silverman, Popper, Patino; 28 Apr 2026), every wallet active Jan 2025 - Apr 2026: **> 100,000 wallets lost ≥ $1,000, nearly twice as many as gained ≥ $1,000**; everyday users collectively lost **$131 million**; a cluster of high-frequency bot-like wallets (method from academic researchers) — **~800 of which made > $100K each** — took roughly **$131M** in profits, mirroring retail losses; bots enter earlier at better prices; retail often picks outcomes correctly but trades late at worse odds; even casual users with < $10 at stake mostly ended negative. — [Newser summary of Bloomberg](https://www.newser.com/story/388532/polymarkets-biggest-winners-arent-human.html); [Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/fintech/100000-polymarket-wallets-lost-at-least-1000-bloomberg-analysis-shows/); original [Bloomberg](https://bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-28/most-prediction-market-traders-are-losing-money-while-bots-rack-up-gains) (paywalled)
- Bloomberg also reported **bots = ~5% of wallets but ~75% of trading volume**, and that "even among the bots... a majority lost money", but the winners' gains were large enough that bots as a group came out ahead. — [Search summary of Bloomberg/Edge coverage](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/801797) (secondary)
- Andrey Sergeenkov (independent on-chain researcher; data to 1 Apr 2026; reported by The Defiant 6 Apr 2026): 2.5M wallets, CTF Exchange + NegRisk CTF Exchange, all USDC flows incl. splits/merges/redemptions; **84.1% lost money**, < 16% any positive return; only 2% > $1,000 cumulative profit; 0.32% > $10,000; **840 addresses (0.033%) > $100,000**. Monthly: 1.25% earned > $1,000/month; 0.26% (~6,600) > $5,000/month; 0.13% (3,250) > $10,000/month. Of the ~6,600 > $5K/month, **53% active only one month; only 2.6% traded continuously > 1 year**. — [TechFlow](https://www.techflowpost.com/en-US/article/31018); [The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/research-and-opinion/polymarket-profitability-report-april-2026) (blocked)
- DeFi Oasis (defioasis.eth, Dec 2025): 1.7M addresses, 124M transactions; ~70% realised losses; **< 0.04% of addresses captured > 70% of realised profits (~$3.7B)**. — [TechFlow](https://www.techflowpost.com/en-US/article/31018); [Tangem summary](https://tangem.com/en/news/markets/7806-polymarket-profits-70-lose-tiny-elite-take-3-7b/)
- Galaxy Research: 69.2% of **retail** accounts (likely bots excluded) below break-even among 2.9M wallets, collectively -$338.9M; international platform only. — [Bonus.com](https://www.bonus.com/news/galaxy-research-nearly-7-in-10-retail-polymarket-traders-ended-up-losing-money/) (search snippet only; page blocked)
- Akey/Grégoire/Harvie/Martineau: 70.8% in the red (Nov 2022 - Oct 2025), top 1% of winners 76.5-84.1% of profits (see Q1). — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:cpr:ceprdp:21615); [InGame](https://www.ingame.com/?p=48488)
- Nov 2024 (election peak): Dune dashboards 86% of accounts negative realised PnL; LayerHub ~88% of ~371,000 wallets in the red, ~237,000 lost ≤ $100, only 4,004 wallets (~1%) realised ≥ $1,000; volume farming for airdrop noted as a cause of many tiny losses. — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/290625/most-polymarket-traders-have-lost-money)
- Telonex 15m crypto, 1 week Feb 2026: 63.2% of 46,945 wallets lost (36.8% profitable), pre-fee. — [Telonex](https://telonex.io/research/top-crypto-traders-polymarket-15m)
- WSJ figure via secondary sources: 0.1% of accounts (< 2,000 traders) captured 67% of profit dollars in the previous year. — [Turbine blog citing WSJ](https://www.turbinefi.com/blog/do-automated-trading-bots-make-money-prediction-markets-2026) (secondary, not verified)
- Bot share of fast-market volume: 55-62% (5m/15m), 45% (hourly), 31% (daily/weekly), Dune to Mar 2026. — [Blockchain.news summary of Dune](https://blockchain.news/news/polymarket-fast-markets-volume-bots-fees-analysis)
- Platform scale context: ~$9.8B nominal 30-day volume and ~462,600 monthly active traders (Token Terminal, ~Apr 2026). — [TechFlow](https://www.techflowpost.com/en-US/article/31018)

### Inferences
- Loss-rate estimates are not comparable: 84-88% count all wallets incl. tiny/airdrop-farming ones; 69-71% are academic or retail-only; 63% is one week in one market type. A defensible range for "genuine traders who lose" is ~65-75%.
- Profit concentration is extreme and stable across methods: roughly 1% of winners take ~3/4 or more of profits, and the persistent winners are disproportionately high-frequency, limit-order (maker), automated wallets. However, persistence is low overall (53% of high earners vanish after one month), so most "winners" in any snapshot are lucky or transient.
- Even among bots, most lose (Bloomberg): automation is necessary but not sufficient; execution type (maker vs taker) and timing matter more than prediction accuracy.

### Gaps
- No source gives a fee-adjusted, entity-level (multi-wallet merged) P&L distribution.
- Bloomberg's bot-identification method and exact bot P&L by strategy not accessible.
- Galaxy Research report not read directly.

## Q4. What professional firms do (market makers on Polymarket / Kalshi)

### Takeaway
Professional prop/market-making firms (Susquehanna on Kalshi since 2024; Jump Trading on Kalshi and negotiating performance-linked equity in Polymarket's US platform) act as liquidity providers — continuous two-sided quoting, earning spread and incentive/rebate payments — rather than directional forecasters. Most large prop shops remain cautious due to thin liquidity and legal uncertainty.

### Cited Findings
- Kalshi publicly confirmed Susquehanna International Group (SIG) as its first dedicated institutional market maker in 2024. — [DeFi Rate](https://defirate.com/news/equity-for-liquidity-jump-trading-set-to-take-stakes-in-kalshi-and-polymarket/)
- Jump Trading, per Bloomberg (anonymous sources), set to take minority stakes in Kalshi (fixed) and Polymarket (growing with trading capacity/liquidity supplied to Polymarket's US platform) through market-making agreements; Jump had already been market making on Kalshi; reportedly ~20 employees trading prediction markets. — [DeFi Rate](https://defirate.com/news/equity-for-liquidity-jump-trading-set-to-take-stakes-in-kalshi-and-polymarket/); [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/357519/jump-trading-earn-stakes-polymarket-kalshi?amp=1) (not fetched)

### Inferences
- The professional playbook matches the academic finding (maker > taker): firms are paid (spread + rebates + equity) to provide liquidity and avoid adverse selection, rather than to forecast.
